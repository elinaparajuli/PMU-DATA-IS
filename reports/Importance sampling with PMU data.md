# Rare-event sampling meets synchrophasor data: an untouched frontier

Importance sampling (IS) and related rare-event methods (cross-entropy IS, adaptive mixture IS, subset simulation, splitting, surrogate-assisted IS) can be used in power-system studies with PMU data wherever crude Monte Carlo (MC) is used today. The methods are already proven on the underlying physics. They cut sample counts by **two to three orders of magnitude** in static security analysis and estimate probabilities **below 10^-22** with a coefficient of variation of about 0.0024 from 10,000 samples. What is missing is the PMU link. Across web searches covering reliability/planning, stability and PMU applications, this review found **no paper in any venue that uses PMU or synchrophasor measurements to define, condition or calibrate an IS proposal**. It also found no paper applying IS to a PMU-specific rare event: detector false alarms, loss of observability, extreme state-estimation error or missed false-data-injection (FDI) detection. Three further patterns stand out. First, in 2020-2026 the composite-reliability IS literature moved mostly out of IEEE Transactions into IJEPES, IET GTD and Chinese journals. Second, **essentially zero confirmed 2020-2026 IEEE Transactions papers** apply IS or subset simulation to dynamic stability. Third, PMU-centric papers still validate with crude MC at moderate probabilities (P_FA around 10^-2 to 10^-1), even though operationally relevant rates are far rarer. For a researcher with skills in MC, statistics, optimisation and Python, this is an unusually open space. The main caveat is verification: IEEE Xplore and arXiv could not be fetched directly, so every "absence" claim and several bibliographic details below rest on search snippets and repository records, and must be confirmed with a systematic IEEE Xplore query before publication.

## Reliability and planning already prove the method stack

The rare-event toolbox for composite generation-transmission (G&T) reliability was built in IEEE Transactions on Power Systems (TPWRS) a decade ago and is now standard. González-Fernández, Leite da Silva, Resende and Schilling combined a cross-entropy (CE) optimisation stage with non-sequential MC. Their **auxiliary distribution of component unavailabilities minimises the variance of LOLP/EENS estimators**, and they tested it on IEEE RTS-79, RTS-96 and a Brazilian system (TPWRS 28(4), 2013) ([ISC record](https://manuscript.isc.ac/Inventory/49/8502.htm)). Hua, Bie, Au et al. then brought **subset simulation** to the problem. They define intermediate failure levels through the minimum load-curtailment LP and draw conditional samples by modified Metropolis MCMC. They report it is "significantly more efficient than standard Monte Carlo simulation, especially for simulating rare failure events" (TPWRS 30(2), 2015) ([XJTU record](https://scholar.xjtu.edu.cn/zh/publications/extracting-rare-failure-events-in-composite-system-reliability-ev/)). Splitting entered through cascading blackouts: Kim, Bucklew and Dobson's TPWRS 2013 splitting paper is the reference work, though its volume and issue are unverified ([arXiv 1711.04491, citing it](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.04491)). Shortle, Rebennack and Glover embedded **rare-event simulation inside transmission-capacity expansion** to minimise blackout probability (TPWRS 2014) ([ISC record](https://manuscript.isc.ac/Inventory/49/7731.htm)). For security analysis, Perninge, Lindskog and Söder's IS of injected powers achieved a **2-3 orders-of-magnitude reduction in computation** on the IEEE 39- and 118-bus systems (TPWRS 2012) ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/239766423_Importance_Sampling_of_Injected_Powers_for_Electric_Power_System_Security_Analysis)).

The 2020-2026 work has mainly fought the curse of dimensionality that renewables bring. Independent-Bernoulli and single-Gaussian CE families break down because the zero-variance density becomes multimodal. Mixtures are the answer the field has converged on, in three flavours. The first is Gaussian mixture models for correlated wind and load, now with closed-form parameter updates (Zhao et al., IJEPES 166, 2025) ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0142061525000869)). The second is discrete mixture models fitted by EM to capture joint component outages, tested on RTS-79/96 (Zhao, Hu, Xie, Tang, *Power System Technology* 50(6), 2026) ([PST record](https://epjournal.csee.org.cn/dwjs/en/article/doi/10.13335/j.1000-3673.pst.2023.2036)). The third is physics-informed mixtures with one component per constraint hyperplane. That last line is the most statistically elegant. Owen, Maximov and Chertkov's ALOE estimator handles the union of 5,772 phase-angle-difference constraints in 326 dimensions at **P < 10^-22 with CoV ≈ 0.0024 from n = 10,000** (Electronic Journal of Statistics, 2019) ([Project Euclid](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/electronic-journal-of-statistics/volume-13/issue-1/Importance-sampling-the-union-of-rare-events-with-an-application/10.1214/18-EJS1527.pdf)). Lukashevich and Maximov's adaptive version tunes the mixture weights by convex optimisation and needs "**a few dozens of random samples, compared to thousands required by the plain Monte-Carlo**" ([OSTI](https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/1972176-power-grid-reliability-estimation-via-adaptive-importance-sampling); [arXiv 2105.08753](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.08753)). The OSTI record places it in IEEE Control Systems Letters, vol. 6 (2021/22), while one snippet called it TPWRS, so its venue needs checking. Surrogates entered too. Xu, Korkali, Mili et al. use a bank of polynomial-chaos surrogates to absorb topology changes from branch outages, then **re-evaluate only the tail samples with the full model to remove surrogate bias** (IEEE Trans. Smart Grid 11(2), 2020) ([NSF-PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10157224)). Manso et al. paired quasi-sequential MC with CE-IS for renewable-rich composite systems (IET GTD 18(2), 2024) ([DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/8a534a25bd3845ddae779c82dacce7b9)). The accompanying PUC-Rio thesis notes that rare load-shedding events "substantially increase the computational cost" of MC for large systems ([PUC-Rio thesis](https://www.maxwell.vrac.puc-rio.br/71881/71881.PDF)).

Two points matter for a planning researcher. First, **no 2020-2026 TEP paper in IEEE Transactions whose core contribution is IS was found**. IS appears in planning only as an inner reliability or blackout evaluator. A Penn State project on MC/IS-based Q-learning for multi-stage TEP on PJM exists only as a project page ([PSU project](https://pure.psu.edu/en/projects/efficient-and-scalable-methods-for-multi-stage-transmission-expan/)). Second, the methods have well-documented failure modes. In splitting, a poorly chosen importance function or time step can make the method **slower than crude MC**. Wadman, Crommelin and Zwart showed that a large-deviations rate function fixes this for line-overload splitting, where crude MC needed "tens to millions" of times more samples ([CWI 23702](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/23702); [CWI 23499](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/23499)). Cascading-failure rare-event research peaked around 2013-2018 in TPWRS. The searches did not surface a 2020-2026 Transactions paper on IS or splitting for cascades; recent effort has shifted toward ML-based N-k screening ([arXiv 2410.00796](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.00796)).

## Stability studies still lean on crude MC and surrogates

Dynamic security is where the gap in the Transactions literature is starkest. The searches found **no 2020-2026 IEEE Transactions paper that clearly applies IS, CE-IS or subset simulation to transient, small-signal, frequency or voltage stability**. The closest IEEE Transactions work is Tan, Deka and Zhao (TPWRS 2025). They build a multi-output Gaussian-process emulator to replace time-domain simulation for probabilistic transient stability assessment, test it on IEEE 9-bus and NPCC 140-bus systems, and use it for spatio-temporal sensitivity analysis rather than rare-event estimation ([NSF-PAR 10658132](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10658132)). Stability-specific rare-event work appears only outside Transactions. Liu, Wang and Wang (McGill/Alberta) apply subset simulation to rare transient instability with sensitivity analysis on a modified WSCC 9-bus system. Their group page lists it as an IEEE PES GM 2025 conference paper ([arXiv 2503.02858](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.02858); [group page](https://sites.google.com/view/xiaozhewang/publications)). The same group followed with a Kriging active-learning framework that locates the rare-instability boundary under a limited simulation budget, listed as to appear in IJEPES 2026 ([arXiv 2605.06442](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.06442)). A 2025 IEEE Xplore document applies subset simulation to probabilistic small-signal stability, but its authors and venue are unverified, and the document number suggests a conference proceeding ([IEEE Xplore 11202156](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11202156/)). Sabot, Labeau and Henneaux's probabilistic DSA framework gives statistical rules for choosing how many **biased and unbiased samples** to simulate on the 73-bus RTS, but it remains a preprint ([arXiv 2505.01147](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.01147)).

The reason is partly structural. Transient stability has no closed-form limit state. A design-point (FORM-type) IS proposal therefore needs a differentiable margin such as CCT, a TDS-derived stability index, an energy function or a surrogate. That favours gradient-free subset simulation and Kriging active learning. Small-signal stability is the opposite case. The limit state (minimum damping ratio ≥ ζ_th) is smooth almost everywhere and eigenvalue sensitivities provide gradients, so it is the **most natural target for CE-IS or design-point IS**. Yet probabilistic damping-ratio security indices are still computed with MC, QMC or LHS ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/261423358_A_small-signal_stability_related_probabilistic_security_assessment_of_wind_power_integration_into_power_systems); [Springer 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00202-024-02763-4)). The DTU group made a fair counterpoint: IS needs prior knowledge of the region of interest, which becomes infeasible as that region shrinks. They proposed boundary-focused sampling for DSA database generation instead ([arXiv 1806.01074](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.01074)). This argues for adaptive methods (CE, subset simulation) over a hand-crafted IS density, not against variance reduction as such. Crude MC also remains standard in DSA training-database generation. A 2026 tabular-foundation-model DSA preprint notes that insecure operating conditions are rare under naive MC, so many simulations are needed ([arXiv 2607.16031](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16031v1)). The searches found no frequency-stability IS paper (rare nadir or RoCoF violations) at all.

The bridge to PMU data is close but not yet crossed. Xiaozhe Wang's group publishes both ambient-PMU estimation of the dynamic state Jacobian and load-dynamics parameters ([arXiv 1706.01114](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.01114.pdf); [arXiv 2002.08310](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2002.08310)) and the rare transient-instability papers above, but none of the papers found combines the two. Bayesian frequency-domain modal analysis already produces **posterior uncertainty on frequency and damping from ambient PMU data**, validated on real Nordic measurements ([Liverpool repository](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3003224/1/Bayesian%20Approach%20in%20the%20Modal%20Analysis%20of%20Electromechanical%20Oscillations_Final.pdf)). That posterior is a ready-made nominal distribution for a rare-event estimator of low-damping probability. A further reason to care about uncertainty: in PMU analysis of two RTE oscillation events, **Matrix Pencil, HTLS and ERA returned negative damping while Prony gave near-zero damping** for the same event, so the choice of estimator materially changes the answer ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/388639962_PMU-based_Modal_Analysis_of_Two_RTE_Oscillation_Events)).

## PMU applications hide rare events behind moderate-probability MC

In every PMU application area searched, MC is crude simple random sampling, typically 10^2-10^4 runs, used to estimate RMSE, coverage, detection rates and empirical false-alarm probabilities around 1-10%. Linear probabilistic state estimation propagates PMU/RTU parameter uncertainty by simple random sampling, with about 10^4 samples on a synthetic US system ([arXiv 1812.05052](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.05052); [arXiv 1905.00329](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.00329)). An IEEE Access 2023 multi-mode oscillation estimator was validated with **1,200 MC trials** ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381695479_The_On-line_Estimation_of_Multi-mode_Electromechanical_Oscillations_Using_the_Cascade_Structure_of_Damped-SOGI)). PMU-placement reliability studies simulate random PMU, PDC and link failures with crude or fuzzy MC. Examples are an IFEEC 2021 conference paper on the IEEE RTS ([KWASU](https://kwasuspace.kwasu.edu.ng/handle/123456789/2597)), a Frontiers 2022 study on the West Bengal grid ([DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/2db5745afdfe412ca779d645f9a958ba)) and IET Smart Grid 2023 ([DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/258fe6432ff7410285df71ed453bea8a)). None of these is an IEEE Transactions paper, and **no 2020-2026 Transactions PMU-placement paper using MC was confirmed**. The only routine IS in the PMU literature is the particle filter in dynamic state estimation, which is sequential IS used for tracking, not for estimating rare probabilities ([UCF STARS, Zhou et al., TSG 2015](https://stars.library.ucf.edu/scopus2015/880)).

The deeper point is that **the operationally relevant probabilities are much smaller than those being estimated**. At 30-60 frames per second, one false alarm per day or week implies a per-window false-alarm probability of roughly 10^-5 to 10^-7. With component unavailabilities of about 10^-3 to 10^-4, losing observability, or N-1 observability, of a PMU/PDC network is a rare event. Stealthy FDI near the detectability boundary is a rare missed-detection event. Detector design currently avoids the problem with analytic thresholds. Follum and Pierre set forced-oscillation periodogram thresholds from the periodogram's distribution to meet a target P_FA, with a frequency-dependent threshold because ambient noise is coloured (TPWRS 31(3), 2016) ([PNNL](https://www.pnnl.gov/publications/detection-periodic-forced-oscillations-power-systems)). Later work whitens the ambient data so thresholds can be set without extensive baselining ([PNNL](https://www.pnnl.gov/publications/practical-line-framework-modified-energy-detector-whitening-ambient-pmu-data)). A structurally constrained GLRT for unobservable FDI provides closed-form P_FA and P_D expressions ([arXiv 2003.08715](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.08715)). Those formulas are exactly what IS could validate at small P_FA. Searches combining "importance sampling" with PMU, synchrophasor, false data injection or bad-data detection returned **no direct hits**.

## Non-Gaussian, correlated PMU errors decide which sampler works

Choosing an IS proposal depends on the tail of the nominal distribution, and PMU errors are not the well-behaved Gaussians most simulations assume. Using redundant WECC PMUs, Wang, Zhao, Huang and Diao found the Gaussian assumption "questionable" and the errors potentially thick-tailed (IEEE Trans. Power Delivery 33(6), 2018) ([PNNL](https://www.pnnl.gov/publications/assessing-gaussian-assumption-pmu-measurement-error-using-field-data)). Calibrator-isolated tests found P- and M-class random errors **distinctly non-Gaussian even under ambient conditions** ([arXiv 2103.09285](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.09285)). A µPMU study rejected Gaussianity of voltage angle in **44% of 1-s windows and 100% of 10-s and 60-s windows**, while warning that test power grows with sample size ([arXiv 1803.06230](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.06230)). The evidence is not unanimous. Earlier filtered field data by Brown et al. looked zero-mean Gaussian ([literature review in arXiv 2103.09285](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.09285)). The Cagliari group finds Gaussian adequate for current magnitude but not necessarily for phase angle, and cautions that field data mixes device errors with system dynamics ([IRIS UniCa](https://iris.unica.it/retrieve/58c13406-014a-4949-bbb2-8925a77605f0/1571101936%20coverpage-iris-unica-cover.pdf)). Gaussian-mixture noise models fitted by EM already improve robust estimation on IEEE 118-bus and utility PMU data ([NSF-PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10096215)).

These findings determine which sampler is efficient. For light-tailed errors (Gaussian or GMM) in linear PMU state estimation, the residual is a linear map of the noise. **Mean-shift (exponential-tilting) IS toward the dominating point is near-optimal**, which is the same instanton logic ALOE exploits. With subexponential tails, exponential tilting fails because rare events are driven by one large error rather than many moderate ones. Proposals must then inflate the scale of individual channels (scale mixtures, conditional MC, Asmussen-Kroese-type estimators). Coloured noise turns a window-level false alarm into a path-space event, so IS must tilt the AR/ARMA innovations or use sequential IS rather than tilting i.i.d. frames. The searches found **no tail-index estimates (Student-t, α-stable, Laplace) fitted to PMU field errors**. Without them, any rare-event estimate inherits an unquantified model-risk term.

Generative models raise a related obstacle. PMU generative models are almost all GANs, many from Texas A&M ([arXiv 1908.08180](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.08180)). In IEEE OAJPE 2021, GAN-generated data added to a small real set **improved event-classification accuracy by 2-5%** ([UTSA repository](https://rrpress.utsa.edu/handle/20.500.12588/1822)). GANs have no tractable density, so they **cannot supply the p(x)/q(x) likelihood ratio** IS requires. No normalizing-flow or diffusion generator for PMU time series was found, although physics-informed diffusion for net-load data reportedly beats GANs, VAEs and flows by at least 20% ([arXiv 2406.01913](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.01913)). Normalizing-flow IS is maturing in structural reliability ([RESS 2024, REIN](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/reensy/v242y2024ics0951832023006439.html); [arXiv 2609.21160](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21160)), but no IEEE power-systems Transactions paper using it was found.

## Seven research directions, ranked by tractability

The directions below follow from the gaps above. They are ordered by how quickly a Python-proficient MC/statistics/optimisation researcher could produce a defensible IEEE Transactions contribution. All of them run on open Python power-system tools such as pandapower, PyPSA, ANDES and SciPy (these tools are suggested, not drawn from the notes).

| # | Direction | Rare event | Recommended method | Gap evidence | Target venue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bad-data and FDI detector calibration under realistic PMU noise | P_FA ≤ 10^-6 of chi-square/LNR tests; missed detection of stealthy FDI | Mean-shift IS (Gaussian), mixture/scale IS (GMM, heavy tails), CE | No IS hits for BDD/FDI; GLRT closed forms untested at small P_FA | TPWRS, TSG, TIM |
| 2 | Observability-loss reliability in PMU placement | P(unobservable) or P(N-1 observability lost) at 10^-6 to 10^-9 | Discrete CE-IS with Bernoulli or categorical-mixture proposals ([arXiv 2309.12490](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.12490)) inside placement optimisation | Placement studies use crude or fuzzy MC | TPWRS, TSG |
| 3 | PMU-conditioned small-signal risk | P(min damping < ζ_th) given ambient-PMU mode or state-matrix posterior | CE-IS or design-point IS using eigenvalue sensitivities | Damping indices use MC/QMC/LHS; Bayesian mode posteriors are not used as nominals | TPWRS |
| 4 | Measurement-informed proposals for composite reliability and TEP | LOLP/EENS, line-limit and angle-difference violations | GMM or flow fitted to historical PMU/SCADA states as nominal or CE initialisation; proposal reuse across candidate plans | No measurement-driven IS found in any venue | TPWRS, TSTE |
| 5 | Long-horizon detector false-alarm rates | Event/oscillation false alarms at about one per day or week | Path-space sequential IS or splitting on AR/ARMA innovations | Thresholds come from analytic or Gaussian theory under coloured noise | TPWRS, TSG |
| 6 | Surrogate + IS for transient and frequency stability with PMU-calibrated models | Rare instability; nadir/RoCoF violations | NN/GP surrogate as proposal or control variate, with TDS tail correction for unbiasedness | Only subset simulation and Kriging (conference/IJEPES); no frequency IS found | TPWRS |
| 7 | Likelihood-based PMU generative models and a rare-event benchmark | Any of the above | Normalizing flows or diffusion as tractable nominals; public ground-truth small-probability tasks | GANs lack densities; pmuBAGE covers events, not rare-event probabilities ([arXiv 2204.01095](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.01095)) | TSG, OAJPE |

Directions 1 and 2 are the quickest wins. The physics is linear or combinatorial, ground truth is checkable against exact or very long MC runs, and both connect to an existing optimisation problem (detector thresholds, PMU placement). Directions 3 and 4 have the most leverage for a transmission-planning profile. They turn PMU archives into the nominal distribution of a planning-grade risk estimate, which no paper found has done. Every direction must handle three methodological hazards found in the literature. The first is CE "cold start" and degeneracy when no initial samples fail, for which Safe-ICE-style heavy-to-light mixtures exist ([arXiv 2509.07160](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.07160)). The second is surrogate bias in the tails, which needs high-fidelity correction as in Xu et al. ([NSF-PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10157224)). The third is reporting. Papers should report both variance-reduction ratio and **work-normalised (CPU-time) efficiency**, because CE pilot runs and OPF/TDS calls dominate cost and the two ratios can diverge widely. If the nominal p is itself learned from PMU data, any rare-event estimate is relative to that model. Tail misspecification then becomes the main error source, and a sensitivity or robustness analysis should accompany the estimate.

## Conclusion

The question is less whether IS can be used with PMU data and more why nobody has done it. The likely answer is that the two communities estimate different things. Rare-event specialists work with model-based input distributions of loads and renewables. PMU researchers report moderate-probability metrics that crude MC handles comfortably, and use Gaussian analytic formulas for the rest. PMU data can enter rare-event estimation in two distinct roles: as the **nominal distribution**, through measured operating states, modal posteriors and empirical noise models; and as the **object of the rare event**, through detector false alarms, observability loss and estimation failure. Each role supports a separate research programme.

The most important insight for a researcher entering the area is that **the PMU error model, not the sampler, sets the ceiling on accuracy**. Mixture and CE methods are mature enough to reach 10^-6 or even 10^-20 cheaply, but those numbers mean little if the tail of the nominal distribution is assumed Gaussian when field data says otherwise. A credible first paper therefore pairs an efficient estimator, benchmarked by work-normalised variance against crude MC, with an empirically grounded tail model and a sensitivity analysis to its misspecification. That combination of MC efficiency, statistical tail modelling and an optimisation outer loop matches the stated skill set, and no confirmed IEEE Transactions paper covers it.
